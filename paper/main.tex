\title{Tumor-Aware Conditional Pix2PixHD with Per-Image Normalization for Virtual Contrast Enhancement in Breast MRI}
\titlerunning{Tumor-Aware Conditional GAN for Virtual Contrast Enhancement}

\begin{abstract}
Dynamic contrast-enhanced MRI (DCE-MRI) is essential for breast cancer diagnosis but requires gadolinium-based contrast agents that pose safety and accessibility concerns. We present a conditional generative adversarial network for synthesizing post-contrast breast MRI from pre-contrast T1-weighted acquisitions. Our method introduces three key contributions: (1) per-image z-score normalization that eliminates cross-scanner intensity variation, improving perceptual similarity by 25\%; (2) a predicted tumor mask as conditional input that provides the generator with an explicit spatial prior for enhancement localization, improving downstream segmentation Dice by 14\%; and (3) a multi-scale enhancement consistency (MSEC) loss that enforces physically plausible enhancement patterns across multiple spatial scales. On our internal validation set, the method achieves a tumor-region SSIM of 0.509, a downstream segmentation Dice of 0.624, and a 95th-percentile Hausdorff distance (HD95) of 69.4, demonstrating that tumor-aware conditional synthesis can produce clinically meaningful virtual contrast enhancement.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% ============================================================================
Dynamic contrast-enhanced MRI (DCE-MRI) plays a central role in breast cancer screening, diagnosis, and treatment monitoring~\cite{mann2019breast}. The administration of gadolinium-based contrast agents (GBCAs) enables visualization of tumor vascularity through characteristic enhancement patterns. However, GBCAs introduce concerns regarding patient safety, including nephrogenic systemic fibrosis in patients with renal impairment and gadolinium deposition in neural tissue~\cite{kanda2014gadolinium}. Additionally, contrast-enhanced protocols increase examination time, cost, and environmental impact through pharmaceutical waste.

Virtual contrast enhancement (VCE) aims to synthesize post-contrast images from non-contrast acquisitions, potentially reducing or eliminating the need for contrast agent administration. The MAMA-SYNTH 2026 challenge~\cite{mamasynth2026} provides a standardized benchmark for evaluating such methods, requiring synthesis of post-contrast subtraction images from pre-contrast T1-weighted breast MRI.
This task presents two fundamental challenges. First, MRI intensity values are not standardized across scanners, institutions, and acquisition protocols, leading to large inter-patient variation that confounds learning-based methods. Second, contrast enhancement is spatially heterogeneous --- tumors exhibit strong focal enhancement while normal tissue shows subtle background uptake --- making it critical for synthesis models to correctly localize and modulate enhancement intensity.

We propose a conditional Pix2PixHD framework that addresses both challenges through three key innovations:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Per-image z-score normalization}: Each image is independently normalized using breast-region statistics, creating a scanner-invariant canonical space for the generator.
\item \textbf{Tumor-aware conditional input}: A predicted tumor segmentation mask is provided as an additional input channel, giving the generator explicit spatial information about where enhancement should occur.
\item \textbf{Multi-scale enhancement consistency (MSEC) loss}~\cite{yang2026msec}: A loss term that enforces consistency between the synthesized enhancement pattern and the ground truth across multiple spatial scales.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Related work.}
GAN-based image-to-image translation has been widely adopted for medical image synthesis. Pix2PixHD~\cite{wang2018pix2pixHD} introduced multi-scale discriminators for high-resolution synthesis. Yang et al.~\cite{yang2026msec} proposed the MSEC loss for breast DCE-MRI synthesis from multi-parametric inputs. Our work differs by operating in a single-modality setting (T1w only), introducing per-image normalization for multi-institutional data, and leveraging predicted tumor masks as spatial conditioning.

\section{Method}
% ============================================================================
\subsection{Overview}
Our pipeline consists of three stages: (1) segmentation of anatomical structures, (2) per-image intensity normalization, and (3) conditional image synthesis with test-time augmentation (TTA). Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline} illustrates the complete training and inference pipeline.

Given a pre-contrast T1-weighted MRI slice $x \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W}$, we first obtain a binary breast mask $m_b$ and a predicted tumor mask $m_t$ using trained segmentation models. The image is then normalized to a canonical intensity space using breast-region intensity statistics. A conditional generator synthesizes the post-contrast image from the three-channel input $[x_{\text{norm}}, m_b, m_t]$, and the result is de-normalized and composited with the original background.

\subsection{Per-Image Z-Score Normalization}
The multi-institutional dataset is provided in a global z-score space, where all images are normalized using dataset-level mean and standard deviation computed over the entire training set. While this removes absolute intensity differences, substantial inter-scanner variation remains across institutions (Table~\ref{tab:dataset}). We therefore apply a second, per-image normalization using each image's own breast-region statistics:
\begin{equation}
x_{\text{norm}} = \frac{x - \mu_{\text{img}}}{\sigma_{\text{img}}}
\end{equation}
where $x$ is the pre-contrast image within the breast region, $\mu_{\text{img}}$ and $\sigma_{\text{img}}$ are the mean and standard deviation computed over breast foreground pixels ($m_b > 0$). This places all breast pixels in a canonical space (mean=0, std=1) regardless of the original scanner characteristics, while background pixels are set to zero. 

To maintain consistency with this per-image normalization scheme, the post-contrast ground truth target is normalized using the \textbf{same pre-contrast image statistics}:
\begin{equation}
y_{\text{norm}} = \frac{y - \mu_{\text{img}}}{\sigma_{\text{img}}}
\end{equation}
This ensures that both input and target occupy the same normalized canonical space, enabling the residual formulation to operate on enhancement differences in a scanner-invariant manner. At inference, de-normalization restores the original intensity: $\hat{y} = G(x_{\text{norm}}, m_b, m_t) \cdot \sigma_{\text{img}} + \mu_{\text{img}}$.

\subsection{Tumor-Aware Conditional Input}
A standard 1-channel generator must learn both \textit{where} and \textit{how much} to enhance. We decouple these by providing a predicted tumor mask as spatial prior. A 2D nnU-Net~\cite{isensee2021nnunet} trained on pre-contrast images predicts $m_t$, which is concatenated as the third input channel:
\begin{equation}
\hat{y}_{\text{norm}} = G([x_{\text{norm}}, m_b, m_t])
\end{equation}

This allows the generator to focus on enhancement intensity rather than localization.

\paragraph{Provenance and patient-level separation of the segmentation models.}
Both conditioning masks come from models we trained ourselves for this task rather than external pre-trained checkpoints applied off-the-shelf. The breast segmentor $m_b$ is a 2D nnU-Net distilled from BreastDivider 3D~\cite{rokuss2025breastdivider}; the tumor segmentor $m_t$ is a 2D nnU-Net trained on pre-contrast T1w slices with radiologist-verified tumor annotations as targets. Critically, the tumor segmentor was built \emph{exclusively} from the training split: its nnU-Net dataset was constructed from the same 9,504 train-split slices used for the GAN, and none of the 60 held-out test patients contribute any slice to it. The patient-level separation asserted for the evaluation segmenter (Sec.~\ref{subsec:experiment_setting}) therefore holds identically for the $m_t$-predicting model, and the conditioning input at test time is never informed by ground-truth annotations of test patients. On its own held-out validation fold (1,901 slices from training-cohort patients, nnU-Net's internal 5-fold split) the tumor segmentor reaches a foreground Dice of 0.767.

To further verify that the reported gains are not an artifact of annotation leakage, we repeated the final configuration with ground-truth tumor masks substituted for predicted ones at test time. This oracle variant changes downstream Dice only marginally (0.624 $\to$ 0.627) and leaves MSE, LPIPS, and FRD essentially unchanged (1.127, 0.123, 27.04). Since even perfect localization adds almost nothing, the improvement credited to tumor-aware conditioning stems from the conditioning mechanism itself rather than from privileged access to test-set annotations. This also confirms that the design is robust to imperfect predictions: approximate localization is sufficient.

\subsection{Network Architecture}
We adapt the GlobalGenerator from Pix2PixHD~\cite{wang2018pix2pixHD}: four strided convolutional layers (ngf=64) compress the $512 \times 512$ three-channel input into a $32 \times 32$ representation, 12 residual blocks model non-linear enhancement patterns, and four transpose convolution layers decode back to full resolution with Instance Normalization throughout.

The generator operates in residual mode, predicting only the enhancement difference map $\Delta$:
\begin{equation}
\hat{y}_{\text{norm}} = x_{\text{norm}} + \Delta
\end{equation}
This residual formulation ensures anatomical preservation by construction, avoiding wasted capacity on reconstructing the static background.

We employ a multi-scale PatchGAN discriminator~\cite{wang2018pix2pixHD} with two branches at full and half resolutions (3 layers each).

\subsection{Training Strategy}
\paragraph{Dataset and preprocessing.}
We evaluate our method using multicenter clinical data. The primary cohort is sourced from the MAMA-MIA dataset~\cite{muller2022mama} provided by the MAMA-SYNTH 2026 challenge~\cite{mamasynth2026}. To maintain spatial orientation consistency with the official challenge test set, we retain only axial acquisitions (DUKE and ISPY2) and exclude sagittal cohorts (DUKE1, ISPY1, and NACT). To enhance model generalization, we supplement with three publicly available cohorts: YUNNAN~\cite{yunnan2024}, AMBL~\cite{ambl2024}, and LA-Breast~\cite{labreast2023}.

To prevent spatial misalignment noise from corrupting the pixel-to-pixel supervisory signal, cases exhibiting significant motion artifacts between pre- and post-contrast phases are excluded. Specifically, we utilize phase correlation to estimate spatial translation and exclude cases with motion displacements exceeding 3 pixels, resulting in only 81 ISPY2 cases removed from the training set, ensuring high spatial alignment between pre- and post-contrast phases.
For temporal alignment and slice selection, we first identify the peak-enhancement phase for each patient, defined as the DCE phase with the highest mean tumor intensity. We then extract the 2D axial slice exhibiting the largest tumor cross-sectional area. To enrich training diversity while preserving local spatial coherence, we additionally extract the four adjacent slices ($\pm 1$ and $\pm 2$ relative to the index slice), yielding five slices per tumor volume. This pipeline results in a total of 9,504 training slices with corresponding ground-truth post-contrast targets. All images and segmentation masks are resampled to $512 \times 512$ using bilinear and nearest-neighbor interpolation, respectively.

\paragraph{Loss Function.}
To train the generator to synthesize anatomically accurate and clinically viable post-contrast scans, we employ a multi-task objective. All loss terms are spatially constrained within the breast parenchyma via the binary breast mask $m_b$, preventing the generator from allocating capacity to clinically irrelevant background regions. The joint objective is:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_G = \lambda_{GAN}\mathcal{L}_{GAN} + \lambda_{feat}\mathcal{L}_{feat} + \lambda_{VGG}\mathcal{L}_{VGG} + \lambda_{MSEC}\mathcal{L}_{MSEC} + \lambda_{tumor}\mathcal{L}_{tumor}
\end{equation}

The \textbf{GAN loss} $\mathcal{L}_{GAN}$ uses a least-squares formulation (LSGAN) with multi-scale PatchGAN discriminators ($D_1$ at full resolution, $D_2$ at half resolution) to encourage high-frequency texture synthesis. The \textbf{feature matching loss} $\mathcal{L}_{feat}$ minimizes the $L_1$ distance between intermediate discriminator features of real and synthesized images, stabilizing training. The \textbf{perceptual loss} $\mathcal{L}_{VGG}$ computes feature-level discrepancies using a pre-trained VGG-19 network, guiding the model toward perceptual similarity beyond pixel-level alignment.

To enforce physical and hemodynamic plausibility, the \textbf{multi-scale enhancement consistency (MSEC)} loss~\cite{yang2026msec} operates on subtraction maps (post-contrast minus pre-contrast):
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{MSEC} = \sum_{s=1}^{S} | D_s(\hat{y}_{\text{norm}} - x_{\text{norm}}) - D_s(y_{\text{norm}} - x_{\text{norm}}) |_1
\end{equation}
where $D_s$ denotes spatial downsampling to scale $s$ ($S = 3$ scales: $1\times$, $\frac{1}{2}\times$, $\frac{1}{4}\times$). This forces the generator to capture both fine-grained focal enhancement and global contrast-agent distributions. We adopt this term directly from Yang et al.~\cite{yang2026msec} without modification, including its weighting, and it is active in all configurations reported in Section~\ref{subsec:results}.

Since tumors occupy a small fraction of breast volume, standard losses suffer from spatial class imbalance. The \textbf{tumor-weighted loss} applies focused $L_1$ supervision within the tumor region:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{tumor} = | (\hat{y}_{\text{norm}} - y_{\text{norm}}) \odot m_t |_1
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Data Augmentation.}
We apply additive Gaussian noise ($\sigma \sim U[0.02, 0.05]$) and random horizontal flipping during training.

\paragraph{Optimization.}
We train for 200 epochs with batch size 16, learning rate $3\times10^{-4}$ with linear decay after epoch 100, using Adam optimizer. The hyperparameter $\lambda_{GAN}=1$, $\lambda_{feat}=10$, $\lambda_{VGG}=10$, $\lambda_{MSEC}=50$, $\lambda_{tumor}=10$.

\subsection{Inference Pipeline}

At test time, the inference workflow (Fig.~\ref{fig:inference_pipeline}) proceeds as follows: (1) we acquire the breast mask $m_b$ via a 2D nnU-Net distilled from BreastDivider 3D~\cite{rokuss2025breastdivider}; (2) we predict the tumor mask $m_t$ via nnU-Net on the pre-contrast scan; (3) per-image normalization to the canonical space; (4) 3-channel synthesis with horizontal-flip TTA; (5) de-normalization and background blending.

For TTA, we run the generator on both the original and horizontally-flipped inputs, flip the second output back, and average the two predictions to reduce directional bias. The final output is blended with the original background:
\begin{equation}
y_{\text{out}} = m_b^{\text{soft}} \cdot \hat{y} + (1 - m_b^{\text{soft}}) \cdot x
\end{equation}
where $m_b^{\text{soft}}$ is the breast mask smoothed by a Gaussian filter ($\sigma = 5$ pixels) to eliminate boundary artifacts. Total inference time is $\sim$3s/case on an NVIDIA T4 GPU.

\paragraph{Diffusion baseline.}
The SUB-DDPM numbers in Table~\ref{tab:merged_results} are \emph{not} taken from the challenge organizers' leaderboard: they come from our own adaptation of the subtraction-based conditional diffusion model of Ibarra et al.~\cite{ibarra2025diffusion}, evaluated on the same 299-slice test set used throughout this work. We reuse the authors' released implementation unchanged in its essentials --- the lightweight \texttt{ConditionalUNet} (8.4M parameters), the $x_0$-prediction formulation with a cosine$^2$ noise schedule, the subtraction target $\mathrm{clip}((y-x)/0.5,-1,1)$, and the composite objective of $0.3\times$MAE $+\ 0.6\times$VGG perceptual $+\ 0.15\times$total variation $+\ 0.05\times$MSE. It was trained for 100 epochs with exponential moving averaging, and we report the best checkpoint sampled with 50 denoising steps (250 training timesteps). Our single substantive departure is the input pipeline, discussed next.

Two factors are important for interpreting the size of the reported gap. First, the two models were not trained on identical data volumes: our generator uses 9,504 training slices, whereas this baseline was trained on 1,194, so the comparison is not compute- or data-matched. Second, and more informative, the method's preprocessing assumptions are incompatible with the intensity representation mandated by this benchmark. The original pipeline min-max normalizes each slice to $[0,1]$ before mapping to $[-1,1]$, so the full dynamic range of every slice is preserved and the $\mathrm{clip}((y-x)/0.5,-1,1)$ target rarely saturates. The challenge instead supplies globally z-scored volumes, which we map to $[-1,1]$ by clipping at $\pm 3$; in a test cohort containing intensities up to $\approx 18$, this truncates the bright voxels that carry the enhancement signal, and the subsequent $\times 2$ scaling of the subtraction target saturates whatever survives. The resulting attenuation is a plausible explanation for the very low $\text{SSIM}_{\text{tumor}}$ and high MSE. We therefore read this row not as evidence against the published method or against conditional diffusion in general, but as an indication that per-slice min-max normalization is load-bearing for this family of models and does not transfer directly to a globally z-scored benchmark.

Evaluation metrics include: mean squared error (MSE), learned perceptual image patch similarity (LPIPS)~\cite{zhang2018lpips}, structural similarity in the tumor region ($\text{SSIM}_{\text{tumor}}$)~\cite{wang2004image}, Fr\'{e}chet radiomics distance (FRD), and downstream segmentation quality (Dice coefficient, 95th-percentile Hausdorff distance, HD95) using an independent nnU-Net.

\paragraph{Diffusion baseline comparison.}
As shown in Table~\ref{tab:merged_results}, we compare against our adaptation of SUB-DDPM and detail the progressive ablation of each component. The diffusion baseline scores poorly on this multi-center test set (SSIM 0.029, Dice 0.072, HD95 419.8). As discussed in Section~\ref{subsec:experiment_setting}, we attribute this primarily to the reduced training set and to the mismatch between the method's per-slice min-max normalization and the globally z-scored inputs of this benchmark, rather than to an inherent limitation of conditional diffusion; the original authors report substantially stronger results under their own preprocessing. Our global z-score baseline outperforms it across all metrics.

\paragraph{Per-image normalization}
replaces the traditional global z-score with an image-specific foreground z-score. This strategy reduces LPIPS by 25\% (0.157 $\to$ 0.117) and improves SSIM by 21\% (0.394 $\to$ 0.476), demonstrating that creating a scanner-invariant canonical space is highly effective for perceptual quality. The minor MSE increase (1.101 $\to$ 1.236) reflects the well-known trade-off between pixel-level L2 optimization and perceptual similarity.

\paragraph{Conditional tumor mask input}
provides the largest segmentation and alignment gains, driving a Dice improvement of 14\% (0.539 $\to$ 0.614) and reducing HD95 by 39\% (125.6 $\to$ 76.0). This validates that incorporating tumor localization as an explicit third channel decouples spatial search from intensity modeling, concentrating the generator's capacity on synthetically enhancing the tumor.

\paragraph{Blending and hflip TTA}
provide final refinements. Test-time augmentation averages out high-frequency generation noise, and soft-boundary blending removes spatial artifacts. Together, they achieve a final SSIM of 0.509, a Dice of 0.624, and HD95 of 69.4. Figure~\ref{fig:qualitative} shows qualitative results across three institutional cohorts.

\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
We presented a tumor-aware conditional GAN for virtual contrast enhancement in breast MRI. Per-image normalization provides a 25\% LPIPS improvement; the conditional tumor mask provides a 14\% Dice increase, addressing complementary failure modes. The residual mode prevents hallucinating tumors from imperfect mask predictions.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
MSE is dominated by outliers, and the breast-masked training loss is mismatched with the full-image evaluation protocol. A further limitation concerns the MSEC term: we adopt it with the formulation and weighting established by Yang et al.~\cite{yang2026msec} ($\lambda_{MSEC}=50$, $S=3$ scales) and retain it in every configuration of Table~\ref{tab:merged_results}, so the cumulative ablation does not isolate its individual contribution. The reported gains from per-image normalization and tumor-aware conditioning are therefore measured on top of a fixed MSEC-supervised objective, and we do not claim independent evidence for MSEC's effectiveness in this single-modality setting. An ablation with $\lambda_{MSEC}=0$ is left to future work. Further directions include model ensembling and multi-resolution processing.
